# Offline storage, cash checkout and synchronization

Implemented scope: encrypted offline **cash sales**, carts and product/price snapshots. Customer credit, refunds, cash-shift transitions, purchasing, counts and warehouse movements require online authorization. A local sale is provisional until the server confirms it. See [rollout and validation](../development/offline-rollout.md).

## Preparing and operating a register

1. Sign in, open a cash shift for the terminal/warehouse/currency, and enable the business offline policy as an owner. Offline is disabled by default.
2. Open **Offline cash register**, enter the business/warehouse IDs and currency, choose a local vault name and a separate 15–128 character passphrase, then prepare online. IDs are available in management/API responses. This initial administration interface deliberately exposes the partition identifiers.
3. The register persists an encrypted snapshot, draft cart and outbox. Choose/scan a product, unit and quantity, save the cart line, review the total and confirm physical cash received. Amounts are currency minor units. A local UUID remains the transaction identity through retries and restart.
4. Keep the cash shift open until all offline sales are confirmed. Reconnect using the original account and synchronize. Background sync runs every 15 seconds; local checkout does not wait for network requests. `pending`, `sending`, `synced`, `rejected` and `conflict` are distinct states. Confirmed records retain their server sale IDs.
5. Inspect unresolved operations. Retry preserves the original ID/payload. Owner reconciliation requires current authority, an active cash shift, valid financial facts and globally available stock. Physical cash must be reconciled into that shift before approval. Price conflicts require a business policy explicitly honoring the snapshot; there is no silent repricing, last-write-wins or deletion of rejected cash evidence.

## Permissions and stock risk

A server-issued lease binds business, account, device, warehouse, currency, cash shift, access fingerprint, policy version and immutable price snapshot. Business policy controls 5–480 minute lifetime, sale/lease cash caps, line quantity cap, manual discount cap, cached-stock allowance and changed-price behavior (`reject_changed` or `honor_snapshot`). Only cash is supported; offline credit is explicitly prohibited. Staff without price-write permission receive zero manual-discount allowance.

The initial bounded dataset supports at most **250 active variants**, one currency and **warehouse-root stock** per vault. Larger catalogs fail preparation explicitly. Snapshots do not contain customer profiles or auth credentials. Multiple offline terminals can consume the same apparent stock: cached quantity is an allowance, never a global reservation. Server upload always enforces actual available stock and retains shortages as conflicts, even if online backorders are enabled.

Local access expires at the lease deadline and detects backward clock changes against the last local write. Background/hidden views and explicit logout erase the in-memory decryption key. Expired registers hide product/cart data and block new sales while preserving encrypted evidence. A trusted device clock and operating-system access controls are assumptions; a hostile device owner can alter client code. The server independently checks operation lifetime, current account/membership/scope, policy, shift, amounts and stock. Offline revocations cannot be instantaneous: maximum grant latency is eight hours. Normal upload permits a 24-hour grace after lease expiration for operations recorded within the grant; older records require owner review. Owner review does not invent verification or bypass stock/financial invariants.

## Storage and recovery

| Platform              | Adapter and transaction                                                                                                             |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Web / Expo web        | IndexedDB version 1, strict read/write transaction and revision compare-and-swap; web requests persistent storage where available   |
| Tauri macOS / Windows | App-data `offline.sqlite`, bundled rusqlite, WAL/FULL synchronous mode, atomic conditional insert/update; narrow named IPC commands |
| Expo Android / iOS    | SDK-matched `expo-sqlite`, WAL/FULL, exclusive migration/write transactions; `.web.ts` routes Expo web to IndexedDB                 |

API-origin namespaces separate development/test/production vaults; business/account/device identity remains inside the authenticated encrypted document. Each vault is an AES-256-GCM authenticated encrypted document with a random 96-bit nonce and PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 (600,000 iterations, independent 128-bit salt). Established `@noble/ciphers`/`@noble/hashes` primitives run without Node/native imports in shared code. No account password, bearer token, long-lived browser credential or unencrypted customer profile enters the vault. Losing the separate passphrase means losing access to its encrypted contents; no recovery backdoor exists.

Document schema 2 migrates schema 1 without deleting pending work. Unknown document/SQLite/IndexedDB versions and malformed/tampered ciphertext fail closed. Writes atomically commit cart/outbox or downloaded projection/cursor together; concurrent windows retry revision conflicts, and server idempotency is the final duplicate guard. A write/quota failure must be resolved before handing over goods. Never clear application storage as a troubleshooting step while unsynchronized sales exist.

`OfflineVault.backup()` returns only the encrypted envelope; `OfflineVault.restore()` validates password/schema and requires an unused vault name. These are support integration APIs, not automated remote backups. Backups require the passphrase and matching original-account authorization on reconnect. Browser eviction, private browsing and device loss cannot be eliminated by this implementation. Preserve the SQLite database including WAL through a consistent filesystem/SQLite backup, or use the encrypted envelope API. Automatic purging and remote backup are intentionally absent; the local outbox is capped at 1,000 retained records and requires an operational retention/export workflow before long-term high-volume rollout.

The production web build emits a versioned service worker containing only public application-shell assets. Private API/auth responses are never cached by the service worker. The separately approved future published storefront projection may use explicitly partitioned public caching; it must not include private data or analytics responses. Updates wait for old clients to close; there is no forced activation during checkout. Vite development is not an offline-reload deployment. Native app bundles supply their own shell. Expo web exports have IndexedDB persistence but no equivalent service worker here; use the primary web application for offline browser restart.

## Server processing and downloads

Batches contain up to 20 stable operations. Each operation runs in an authenticated business-locked transaction, with a savepoint around domain application. Permanent domain failures roll back effects and persist an unresolved receipt. The unique business/operation key, actor and canonical-payload hash prevent duplicate application and key reuse with different data. Immutable original payloads are retained; confirmed outcomes cannot be rewritten. Current scope is checked before returning cached results. Partial batches and lost responses can be retried intact.

Upload backoff is exponential with jitter and a five-minute ceiling. Eight failed upload attempts, or permanent authorization failures, require manual retry/review. Requests and local state are separate: a missing response never implies an uncommitted sale. Account mismatch blocks pull/upload before touching a vault's partition.

Stage 4 makes batch claims atomic across windows and persists attempts/backoff before network I/O, including a minimum ten-second claim window matching the mutation transport deadline. A restart respects that window and stops exhausted attempts for manual review. Late failed/conflict replies cannot downgrade a confirmed local result. Manual retry keeps the original operation ID and payload; it cannot make a rejected financial operation valid.

Password recovery and operator account status changes revoke existing server grants through the sync-owned transaction boundary. Disconnected clients cannot discover that immediately; local expiry still limits use to the original lease window. On reconnect, unsynced operations from a revoked grant become retained conflicts for authorized reconciliation. Do not delete the vault or recreate sales to work around revocation.

Delta cursors also bind currency. A currency change or a pre-Stage-4 cursor triggers a full snapshot replacement rather than silently retaining prices from another currency. The migration discards only ephemeral cursor rows, never pending operations or financial records.

Database triggers transactionally increment a per-business revision head; its row lock is held until commit. This avoids skipping a transaction that allocated a sequence before another transaction committed. Opaque UUID cursors are bound to actor, warehouse and access fingerprint, expire after seven days, and are applied atomically with cache changes. Changes return upserts/removals; more than 1,000 change hints, global catalog changes, expired/mismatched cursors and minute-boundary price transitions trigger a bounded full snapshot. Pending operations retain their immutable original leases. The current catalog is bounded rather than an unbounded initial-download protocol.

## Live invalidation

POS screens poll an authenticated, warehouse/store-scoped revision endpoint every three seconds. It returns a version and minute clock bucket, never entity payloads. This polling path predates the catalog WebSocket described below; both are invalidation mechanisms, not business posting channels. Notifications invalidate online quotes and require calculation/review again; they do not execute business operations. Duplicate versions are ignored. Reconnection and missed changes recover through the delta API, independently of notifications. Offline registers download on their sync interval and renew approved snapshots when unresolved operations no longer block renewal. Until renewal, the UI identifies a stale snapshot and the original lease's explicit price policy governs upload. Time-scheduled price changes are detected at minute granularity; authoritative checkout always revalidates immediately.

Online catalog clients additionally subscribe to authenticated WebSocket revision hints. They reread current prices through scoped HTTP endpoints; reconnect triggers a fresh read. This channel never replaces an offline snapshot, replays a financial command or marks an outbox entry synced. See [live pricing protocol](../development/live-ui-refinement.md).

## Automatic outage detection and task feedback (October 2026)

Web/desktop listen for browser offline/online events and probe API readiness every five seconds (five-second request timeout). Native probes readiness on foreground and every five seconds. Empty, unlocked live POS automatically offers the cached checkout when unavailable. A live/uncertain cart is retained rather than silently converted into a different sale. Operators must prepare and unlock the encrypted register beforehand; this is not permission to cache every API or perform arbitrary operations offline. An encrypted vault cannot be unlocked automatically after restart without its separate passphrase. Development Vite is still not an offline-reload deployment.

Unlocked registers synchronize immediately when API readiness returns and retain the existing 15-second recovery polling/backoff. Eligible outbox records drain in batches of 20 using their original IDs and atomic claims. Live progress counts only server-confirmed records; conflicts and rejected/unknown outcomes remain visible. Upload confirmation and catalog refresh are separate phases. The notification menu shows the active local task; completion/attention feedback offers a drawer with operation states, confirmed sale IDs and unresolved errors. Local task notifications disappear on lock and contain no cross-account history. The durable encrypted outbox remains the recovery source.

Transient background session/workspace read failures preserve the previously verified in-memory view only until the original session expiry. This does not renew a session or offline grant. Explicit denials, malformed responses, sign-out, background/visibility lock and expiry retain their clearing behavior. Every remote mutation remains server-authorized. Cash grants still expire independently; credit, refunds, purchasing and inventory mutations remain online workflows. A successful upload is not inferred from network availability or bytes sent.

## Milestone 1 boundaries — approved design, implementation pending

See [D07–D11](../decisions/milestone-1.md). Online orders, approvals, fulfillment, manual bank confirmation, refunds and clearing reconciliation remain online-only. The existing encrypted cash lease does not authorize those operations. Mobile feature parity does not expand grant capabilities.

The future storefront allocates sellable stock as **on hand − reserved − damaged − buffer**; transit remains separate. Backorder quantity is explicitly unallocated, never a fictional hold. Buffers/channel allocations reduce risk but cannot guarantee global stock on disconnected registers. Cached stock remains a bounded allowance. Late uploaded cash sales that cannot satisfy current stock/policy remain retained conflicts for authorized reconciliation; do not silently prioritize one channel, delete evidence, reprice originals or fabricate success. Successful sync creates each server source once with the existing stable operation identity; a reconnect or task progress update does not post money or stock by itself.

Analytics v1 is online-only. During a transient outage, a previously authorized aggregate response may remain **in process memory**, marked “Cached — as of” with its server timestamp. Scope it to account/business/permission fingerprint/query/currency/timezone; clear on expiry, denial, account/business/access change, background/visibility lock, logout or restart. Reauthorize on reconnect. No analytics snapshots in IndexedDB, SQLite, offline cash vaults, backup envelopes, service workers or other persistent caches. This reconciles the spec's last-loaded-data request with the no-generic-API-cache policy; it is not a restart-capable offline reports feature.

The approved future journal-posting intent in D10 is a **server-owned** durable process, not a new device mutation outbox. Financial/stock sources and audit commit transactionally; journal source uniqueness prevents repeat posting. Pending journal work is visible and never presented as posted. Existing cash-outbox claims, lease windows, grace periods, backoff and protected retry IDs remain unchanged.

Qualification uses the [release checklist](../release/qualification.md). Web/Windows/macOS launch requires outage/restart/storage/hardware evidence on the exact artifacts; Android/iOS parity and native qualification are tracked separately. Exports alone do not verify a vault.
