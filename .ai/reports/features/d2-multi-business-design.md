## Recommended decisions

1. **Eligibility:** require the acting user to hold sales permission and relevant store/warehouse access in every participating business, plus payment/customer permissions when applicable. No delegated permission that substitutes for missing tenant access. The phrase “same owner” is a product grouping, not an authorization shortcut. Recommend allowing explicitly authorized staff too; strict common-owner-only eligibility would need a separate verified ownership rule, not matching emails or names.
2. **Bilateral consent:** cross-business collection creates a debt between legal businesses. Require an explicit, versioned collection agreement approved by authorized administrators on both sides. Reuse trading consent infrastructure, but add this purpose explicitly; a generic active trading relationship is not sufficient. Revocation blocks new collections, preserves historical evidence and permits separately authorized settlement/refund of existing obligations.
3. **Customer identity:** select local customer records for each participating store/business; phone lookup is only a suggestion after access checks and requires confirmation. No global customer directory, automatic matching or copied credit limits. Walk-in cash can omit customers. Parent recipient snapshot is a summary identity, not a receivable account.
4. **Currency/location bounds:** one currency per combined checkout, one checkout warehouse per store, as proposed in D1. Multiple stores within a business use its D1 group. Mixed-currency browsing/reports show separate totals, but checkout rejects a mixed-currency cart. No FX or cross-currency netting.
5. **Payments:** cash and existing authorized credit only. Cash is collected once into the collecting business/store's actual open shift. Other businesses record payment via intercompany clearing, not fictitious local till cash. Credit stays with each local customer and is checked independently. No cards/providers or bank-transfer feature expansion here.
6. **Atomicity:** same authoritative PostgreSQL deployment only. All participating businesses, journal periods, credit and stock must validate and commit together. Separate servers/local installations cannot provide this atomic guarantee; do not introduce distributed checkout or claim offline support.
7. **Receipts:** customer gets a combined summary plus issuer-specific receipts, each with immutable identity/number/tax metadata. If multiple stores of one issuer participate, retain D1 store receipts under that issuer's summary. The combined summary is not a second tax invoice. Required jurisdictional tax fields/numbering need product qualification before describing output as legally compliant.
8. **Reports:** optional read-only combined view from authorized existing aggregations, separate currencies and explicit selected-business scope. No statutory consolidated accounts or automatic consolidation/elimination engine. Include pairwise intercompany reconciliation, clearly distinguished from sales totals.

## Ownership and schema proposal

- Use a narrow coordination root (`combined_bills`) with opaque ID, actor, immutable operation payload hash, currency, collection context and lifecycle. It is not owned by an unrestricted “group tenant,” and never grants child access.
- Link tenant-owned D1 bill groups/sales through explicit `combined_bill_participants` with business/store/child identifiers. Enforce composite FKs to the correct tenant; child sale membership is unique. The coordinator validates the entire participant set, not a client-supplied scope bypass.
- Tenant-owned projections contain only that tenant's lines, customer, tax and payments. Full parent projections require current access to every included business/store. Single-business users may read their own child without learning other parties' totals, customers or item counts.
- One collector-owned cash receipt records actual tender/change/applied cash. Local allocations settle the collector's sales; remote allocations create tenant-local collection evidence and reciprocal intercompany postings. Never insert a foreign sale/customer/shift into existing tenant-composite cash FKs.
- Introduce typed non-cash settlement evidence for remote child sales, allocated to their sale/receivable and linked by an opaque shared collection reference. Extend receiptFinance, statements, source posting and refund capacity to recognize that method. Do not mark a remote receivable unpaid merely because it has no local cash-payment row.
- Intercompany counterparties map verified business IDs to locked control accounts/dimensions from Feature C. Every obligation/settlement has matched tenant-local evidence and a shared immutable operation reference; no separately editable outstanding balances.
- Add explicit collection agreement terms/version/state, consent actors and audit. Existing trading agreements/orders retain their current meaning.
- Add validated tax-registration metadata and versioned receipt-number allocation where required. Snapshot issuer identity, address, applicable registration, store reference, taxes and numbering at sale time; later settings edits do not change issued receipts. No fabricated registration defaults/backfills.
- Forward migrations are additive; existing sales remain ungrouped. Backfill only proven references; leave old receipts unchanged. Rollback disables new commands while retaining read/reconciliation support and all posted evidence. Never remove records or constraints to force a downgrade.

## Transaction, authorization and retry contract

1. Bound the request (proposed 5 businesses, 10 stores and 50 lines total); require unique participant/child identities, one currency and one collector. Derive business/store ownership server-side.
2. Open **one** DatabaseService transaction. Acquire all business locks in stable UUID order, then resolve a fresh authorized OrganizationContext per business over the same transaction. Extract the existing access-resolution logic; never mutate one context's business ID or recursively invoke OrganizationScope.run for children.
3. Check every membership/permission, location, customer, collection agreement and period before revealing foreign data or replaying any result. Locks and revocation paths must follow compatible ordering; test checkout against concurrent revocation/settlement/trading operations for deadlocks.
4. Requote every child through its own pricing context. Aggregate only matching-currency exact minor units; fingerprint binds the complete ordered participant set, child quotes, customer mappings, allocations, collector and agreement versions. Prices/taxes are not recomputed by the UI.
5. Lock stock, credit, shifts, counters and period resources deterministically; validate all children and aggregate local customer exposure. Never use another business's credit limit or credit override. A failed participant aborts the whole bill.
6. Persist root, children, stock movements, physical cash, customer allocations, both sides of intercompany journals, numbering, audits and operation receipts before commit. D1/C helpers must accept the same transaction. Neither audit notifications nor rendering may publish partial success externally before commit.
7. One immutable root operation key plus a stable per-business key map survives uncertain responses; each tenant receipt stores only its authorized local result. Server validates the map against root/participant identity and rejects conflicting payload reuse. No child-by-child success/retry commits.
8. A read-only status lookup reauthorizes the full participant set before returning the parent. Permission loss returns a safe denial and recovery guidance; it must not expose an old cached full response. No client auto-creates a new key following timeout, restart or denial.

Require open accounting periods in every participating business for this new command, using each business's posting-date/timezone policy. This is an explicit D1/C decision dependency, not a change to existing standalone checkout. Missing setup is a preflight blocker, and authoritative checks repeat at commit.

## Financial example and settlement rules

Illustrative amounts are minor units, tax omitted for clarity: hardware H sells 1,000; furniture F sells 2,000; coffee C sells 300. H collects 3,300.

- Each business posts its own sale: Dr customer settlement/AR, Cr its revenue/tax using the existing exact quote split.
- H's own receipt allocation: Dr cash(H) 1,000 / Cr settlement(H) 1,000.
- H's remote collection allocations: Dr cash(H) 2,300 / Cr intercompany payable-to-F 2,000 / Cr payable-to-C 300.
- F: Dr intercompany receivable-from-H 2,000 / Cr customer settlement(F) 2,000. C records the analogous 300. Every business's journal balances independently.
- Only H's actual till increases, by 3,300. F/C customer payments are confirmed clearing allocations, not local cash. Parent creates no revenue and no second payment.
- Cash settlement H→F 2,000: H Dr payable-to-F / Cr cash(H); F Dr cash(F) / Cr receivable-from-H. Both shifts must be authorized/open, sufficient source cash available, and both records/postings commit atomically. Support partial settlement; reject over-settlement and mismatched currency.
- If F's customer instead owes 500, F's intercompany claim is only the collected 1,500; its customer retains 500 AR. Later cross-business collection uses the same authorized agreement, allocation and journal path. Settlement must not credit customer AR a second time.
- Intercompany pairs reconcile by shared reference, counterparty and currency. Aggregate views may show matched amounts but must not silently hide mismatches, pending sources or one-sided legacy records. No automatic netting between unrelated counterparties.

## Refunds, voids and lifecycle

- Parent commit is all-or-nothing; no externally visible partially committed state. UI: editing → quoting → review → submitting → confirmed or uncertain/status recovery. Historical summary state derives from child returns/voids.
- Child return uses original quantities, cumulative rounding, disposition, credit-before-refund rules and appropriate refund approval. Never use another business's tax or stock location.
- For remote paid amounts, recommend refund through the original collector's currently open authorized shift. Both businesses must authorize; seller issues its return/credit and reverses clearing, collector pays actual cash and reduces/reverses its payable. If previously settled, create the reverse obligation explicitly; never delete or undo settled evidence.
- Collector unavailable/permission missing/insufficient cash: do not claim refund paid. Block that atomic cash-refund command with clear recovery guidance. Supporting a separately posted return followed by a pending payout is a further workflow decision, not an implicit change to current coupled returns.
- Cross-business refunds require both parties' approval policies; use the stricter applicable requirements without self-approval or split-request bypass. If approval queue support is incomplete, record it as an implementation prerequisite.
- Full-parent void is not a blind destructive action. Expose per-child authorized correction and derive parent status. Journal reversal alone cannot undo a sale or collection.

## API and user experience proposal

Proposed coordination routes use `/combined-bills`, outside a misleading single-business checkout route, with explicit authenticated participant resolution. Existing routes/contracts remain supported.

| Surface                                       | Proposed behavior                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /combined-bills/eligible-contexts`       | Active accessible businesses/stores with sales permission and collection readiness; no global business directory.                                  |
| `POST /combined-bills/search`                 | Bounded selected contexts, query, cursor; tenant-scoped product results labeled by business/store/currency. No N unbounded client catalog fetches. |
| `POST /combined-bills/quote`                  | Child quotes and payment allocation preview; no reservation or posting.                                                                            |
| `POST /combined-bills`                        | Atomic checkout with root and participant keys.                                                                                                    |
| `GET /combined-bills/:id`, `/operations/:key` | Fully authorized details/result recovery; local child reads remain on existing tenant endpoints.                                                   |
| Intercompany settlement command               | Counterparty, exact source obligations, currency, amount and actual till contexts; authorized in both businesses with a stable command key.        |
| Consolidated report read                      | Explicit selected accessible businesses, period/timezone/currency/filter fingerprint, bounded pages/export and as-of marker.                       |

- Reuse `/pos` with an explicit “Multiple businesses” scope, business/store badges and grouped subtotals. Line identity includes business/store/warehouse/variant/unit/location/discount; identical SKU labels across businesses never merge.
- Review shows collector, per-issuer totals/tax, local customer mappings, cash allocation and credit before one confirmation. Barcode collisions require explicit selection. Scope changes never silently relocate or discard lines.
- POS completion shows summary and child receipt actions; Sales & receipts gives full-parent links only to fully authorized users. Accounting exposes intercompany balances and settlement drawers using existing components/native equivalents.
- No new broad authority permission replaces sales/payment/location checks. Agreement management requires both businesses' existing administration authority; settlements require cash and accounting authority on both sides. If new narrow capability names are needed, add role defaults/server enforcement/UI gates/audit together.
- Notifications contain tenant-local evidence only. Full financial rows, customer phone numbers and hidden-party details must not leak through errors, agreement labels, audit payloads, status lookups, exports or WebSocket invalidation.
- Reports include only selected businesses for which the user has required report/finance access. Loss of one participant fails the current requested report and asks for a new selection; never silently return a partial total under the old label. Apply each business's timezone with explicit boundaries; currencies remain separate. Cache only authorized in-memory as-of data under current security rules.
- All platforms share coordination contracts/models and semantic tokens; native uses native panels/tabs. Preserve density/font/radius/motion preferences and keyboard/scanner handling. No new component library or production PDF dependency is authorized by this design.
- States: no eligible businesses/agreement, loading/no results, denied, changed quote, mixed currency, missing issuer setup, closed period/shift, credit denial, offline, submitting, uncertain, confirmed, print failure, unsaved changes and correction confirmation. Showcase/mock fixtures must not mutate live data.
- Online means every participant is served by the same reachable authoritative API/database. Never split a group into offline outbox sales. Preserve an in-memory unsubmitted draft during brief loss; session lock/expiry clears sensitive data according to current rules. Cross-restart recovery requires the approved safe command identity approach from A/D1, not plaintext cart storage.

## Risks, alternatives and delivery sequence

- **Reject common ownership as automatic access:** an account's presence in several businesses does not establish permission to read/write every location or act as collecting agent.
- **Reject a global shared customer/credit limit:** merges tenant data and financial obligations. Use confirmed tenant-local mappings.
- **Reject independent tenant HTTP checkouts or distributed saga for v1:** cannot meet no-partial-bills acceptance. Only one database transaction qualifies.
- **Reject fake local cash payments for remote sellers:** inflates tills and breaks tenant FKs/refunds. Add real clearing settlement evidence and update every consumer.
- **Reject automatic trading-order financial posting:** changes existing coordination-only behavior. Collection agreement and sale sources are explicit additions.
- **Reject parent revenue or a parent legal tax invoice:** duplicates issuer effects. Summary plus issuer receipts only.
- **Blocker:** C and D1 have not been approved/implemented; their names are design dependencies, not available intercompany functionality. Missing tax-registration schema/numbering also prevents declaring legal-receipt readiness.

Recommended order after approval:

1. **L:** C/D1 chart, attribution, source uniqueness, transaction-local helpers, recovery and receipt foundations; resolve period/credit/refund decisions.
2. **L:** cross-business coordinator, lock order, explicit consent and tenant-local payment/clearing schema; database authorization/rollback tests before UI.
3. **L:** atomic checkout, partial settlement and post-settlement refund; statement/report/receipt consumer updates and reciprocal reconciliation.
4. **M:** shared POS grouping, native/web/desktop UI, reporting and safe exports with meaningful state fixtures.
5. **L:** end-to-end and device qualification, performance bounds and legal-receipt product review.

Acceptance tests: hardware+furniture+coffee example; user missing one tenant/store; membership/consent revocation during submission and replay; invalid child customer; independent credit limits and overrides; tax/price differences; mixed currency; same SKU across tenants; remote cash not counted locally; duplicate root/child keys; failure on last child rolls everything back; deadlock/concurrent settlement; closed periods/shifts; partial settlement and refund after settlement; privacy of partial access; both-side audits; receipt identity immutability; unchanged single-store/offline/trading behavior. Use isolated real PostgreSQL, not mocks, for atomicity and constraints.
